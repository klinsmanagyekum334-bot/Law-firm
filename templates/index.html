KNOW YOUR RIGHTS

The 1992 Constitution protects you. Few people read it.

Most of our clients tell us the same thing: "I didn't know I could do that." A few basics worth knowing before you ever need us:

  • If you are arrested, you must be told why, and you must be brought before a court within 48 hours (Article 14).
  • You can choose your own lawyer and you are presumed innocent until a court says otherwise (Article 19).
  • Before you pay for land, search it at the Lands Commission. Many people lose money to double sales that a simple search would have caught.
  • If someone dies without a will, the Intestate Succession Law, 1985 (PNDCL 111) decides how the estate is shared between the spouse, children, parents and extended family.

This is general information and not advice on your own case. Every situation is different, so please speak to us.

Questions people ask us

Is the first consultation really free?

Yes. We would rather you understand your position before spending any money.

What should I bring to the first meeting?

Any document linked to the matter: a court notice, a lease or sale agreement, a police report, your ID. If you don't have anything yet, come anyway.

Will what I tell you stay private?

Yes. What you tell your lawyer is confidential, and we do not share it without your permission.

Can we settle without going to court?

Often, yes. Mediation under the Alternative Dispute Resolution Act, 2010 (Act 798) can save time and money, and we will tell you honestly when it is worth trying.

How long will my case take?

It depends on the court and the type of case. Some matters are settled in weeks, others take longer. We will give you a realistic estimate after reading your papers.
