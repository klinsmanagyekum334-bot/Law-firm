Privacy & Client Confidentiality

Last updated: 2026

When you contact a lawyer you often share the most private details of your life. This page explains in plain words what we collect, why we collect it and how we protect it.

Your lawyer's duty of confidentiality

Everything you tell us in order to get legal help is confidential. This duty comes from the lawyer's professional rules and from the legal protection given to communications between a lawyer and client. We do not discuss your matter with anyone outside the firm without your permission, except where the law or a court requires it.

What information we collect

We collect only what we need to help you: your name, phone number, email and address, details of your matter, and any documents you give us, such as agreements, court papers, ID or police reports. If you visit our website, our hosting may record basic technical details such as the pages visited.

How we use it

We use your information to understand your problem, give advice, act for you, keep you updated and meet our legal and professional obligations. We do not sell your information, and we do not use it for advertising.

How we protect it

Access to your file is limited to the lawyers and staff working on your matter. We ask for only the documents we need, we keep your papers away from public view, and we ask staff to keep client matters confidential. If you prefer a particular way of being contacted, for example by phone only, please tell us and we will respect it.

Who we may share it with

We share information only when it is necessary for your case, for example with a court, a registry or office, an expert, or another party's lawyer, and then only the details required. We will tell you before we do so wherever possible.

How long we keep it

We keep your file for as long as it is needed for your matter and for the period lawyers are expected to keep records. After that, it is closed and stored or securely destroyed.

Your rights

Under the Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843), you have rights over your personal information, including the right to ask what we hold about you and to ask us to correct it. To make a request, contact us using the details below. The Data Protection Commission is the national body that oversees these rights.

Using WhatsApp, email and phone

These are convenient for booking and general questions, but they are not always private. Please avoid sending sensitive documents or detailed accounts of your case through them until we have agreed to act for you and told you the best way to send them.
